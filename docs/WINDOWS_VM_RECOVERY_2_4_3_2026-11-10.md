## Observed failure and correction

The failed session reported `Independent Windows audio guardian did not report readiness`, followed by repeated HLS watchdog recoveries and `Repeated A/V stalls; video-only fallback to maintain desktop control`. A read-only observation at 21:38:01.852 UTC found the playlist unchanged during a 109 ms status request: the fresh inspector measured its age as 943 ms, while the running cast process reported 7,837 ms. The inferred process clock difference was 6,894 ms. Further observations showed offsets changing sign. The initial inspector then encountered a missing playlist during another encoder restart; the subsequent bounded collector retained its samples separately.

The application compared timestamps from independently running Node processes and the filesystem. [V8's Windows clock implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nodejs/node/v24.15.0/deps/v8/src/base/platform/time.cc) resamples a process-local clock periodically. [The bundled Node/libuv Windows uptime implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nodejs/node/v24.15.0/deps/uv/src/win/util.c) uses `GetTickCount64`. These sources explain why wall-clock comparisons can disagree; they do not establish the underlying cause of the VM's clock adjustments.

The targeted changes are:

- Audio guardian readiness and recovery leases use the same boot-relative Windows uptime clock in both processes. Records identify their clock basis. Expired, future, wrong-session and old-clock readiness is refused. Old recovery journals and a clock reset still take the existing endpoint-checked restoration path.
- The video watchdog measures elapsed time since it last observed a playlist update, rather than subtracting a filesystem timestamp from its own wall clock. Real freezes, startup failures, recovery limits and owner Stop remain guarded. Human-readable event timestamps remain UTC.
- The TV audio-route request allows 20 seconds for the existing bounded guardian/native operations. Previously its 5.5-second timeout could expire before the guardian's permitted nine-second startup wait. Other browser requests retain their existing timeout.

No FFmpeg, Node, native helper, discovery protocol, TV identity or host networking-selection logic was changed. The new clock module is included in the portable payload allowlist. Installer/app identities distinguish the new package; no installed payload was patched in place.
